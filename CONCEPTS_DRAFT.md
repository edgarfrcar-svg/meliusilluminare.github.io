# Melius Illuminare — Concept Drafts
## Traditional & Apostolic Register

All entries are written in the voice of the Latin tradition: Scripture (Douay-Rheims), the Fathers, the Doctors, the Councils. No inter-confessional polemic. The aim is clarity, humility, and fidelity to the public teaching of the Church.

---

## 1. Grace — *Gratia*
**Domain:** Salvation

**Short**  
The free, unmerited gift by which God heals and elevates the human person to share His own life.

**Definition**  
Grace is the free and unmerited gift of God by which the human person is called, healed, elevated, and made a partaker of the divine nature. It is not a wage owed to nature, nor a mere external favor, but God’s own action toward the creature — prior to every good movement of the will, and ordered to supernatural communion with Him.

**Apostolic note**  
“By grace you are saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, for it is the gift of God” (Ephesians 2:8). “Grace does not destroy nature, but perfects it” (Aquinas, ST I, q. 1, a. 8 ad 2).

## 2. Justification — *Iustificatio*
**Domain:** Salvation

**Short**  
The translation of the sinner from the state of sin into the state of grace and adoption as a child of God.

**Definition**  
Justification is not the remission of sins merely, but also the sanctification and renewal of the inward man, through the voluntary reception of the grace and of the gifts, whereby man of unjust becomes just, and of an enemy a friend. The formal cause is the justice of God — not that by which He is just, but that by which He makes us just.

**Apostolic note**  
“By one man’s obedience many shall be made just” (Romans 5:19). Trent Session VI is the solemn map: five causes, faith as root, charity as form, baptism as instrument, loss and restoration real.

## 3. Eucharist — *Eucharistia*
**Domain:** Sacraments / Worship

**Short**  
The Body and Blood of Christ, truly, really, and substantially present under the appearances of bread and wine; the memorial of His Passion and the food of eternal life.

**Definition**  
The Eucharist is the sacrament in which Christ Himself is present, offered, and received. It is both sacrifice and banquet: the same Victim who offered Himself on the Cross is offered in an unbloody manner on the altar, and the faithful are fed with His Body and Blood for the remission of sins and the increase of charity.

**Apostolic note**  
“This is my body… This is my blood of the new testament” (Matthew 26:26–28). “Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood, you shall not have life in you” (John 6:54). Trent, Session XIII: real presence, transubstantiation, the Mass as true sacrifice.

## 4. Trinity — *Trinitas*
**Domain:** God

**Short**  
The one God who is Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — three Persons, one substance, equal in majesty.

**Definition**  
We worship one God in Trinity, and Trinity in Unity, neither confounding the Persons nor dividing the substance. The Father is made of none, the Son is of the Father alone, the Holy Spirit is of the Father and the Son. None is before or after another; none is greater or less than another. The whole three Persons are co-eternal and co-equal.

**Apostolic note**  
“Go therefore and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost” (Matthew 28:19). The Athanasian Creed and the Cappadocian settlement guard the mystery against both Sabellius and Arius.

## 5. Atonement — *Redemptio / Satisfactio*
**Domain:** Salvation

**Short**  
The work of Christ by which He reconciles humanity to God through His Passion and death.

**Definition**  
Atonement names the satisfaction offered by the Word made flesh for the sin of the world. Christ, true God and true man, freely offered Himself to the Father as a sacrifice of infinite value. By His obedience unto death He merited the grace of justification for all, and opened the way of return to the Father.

**Apostolic note**  
“He was wounded for our iniquities, he was bruised for our sins” (Isaiah 53:5). “Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures” (1 Corinthians 15:3). Anselm’s *Cur Deus Homo* and the Thomistic account of satisfaction remain the classic Latin grammar; the Cross is both victory and propitiation.

## 6. Church — *Ecclesia*
**Domain:** Ecclesiology

**Short**  
The Body of Christ, the people of God, the pillar and ground of the truth — one, holy, catholic, and apostolic.

**Definition**  
The Church is the congregation of all the faithful who, being baptized, profess the same faith, partake of the same sacraments, and are governed by their lawful pastors under one visible head, the successor of Peter. She is the ordinary ark of salvation; outside her there is no ordinary means of salvation, yet God is not bound to the visible sacraments in such a way that He cannot save those who, through no fault of their own, do not know her.

**Apostolic note**  
“Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church” (Matthew 16:18). “The house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth” (1 Timothy 3:15). Cyprian: “He cannot have God for his Father who has not the Church for his mother.”

## 7. Free Will — *Liberum Arbitrium*
**Domain:** Anthropology / Grace

**Short**  
The rational appetite by which man is master of his own acts — wounded by sin, healed and elevated by grace, never abolished.

**Definition**  
Free will is the power of the will to choose among goods under the light of reason. After the Fall the will remains free, yet it is inclined to evil, darkened, and weakened. Grace does not destroy this freedom; it heals and moves the will so that it freely consents. God causes not only the act, but the free mode of the act.

**Apostolic note**  
“It is God who worketh in you, both to will and to accomplish” (Philippians 2:13). Orange and Trent refuse both Pelagius and any doctrine that would make grace a coercion. The will remains a will.

## 8. Original Sin — *Peccatum Originale*
**Domain:** Anthropology / Fall

**Short**  
The inherited deprivation of original justice and the wounding of human nature that follows from Adam’s sin.

**Definition**  
Original sin is the sin of Adam transmitted by propagation, not by imitation. By it the whole human race lost original justice — the gift by which the lower powers were ordered to reason and reason to God. The person is not a beast, but he is not whole: inclined to evil, darkened in mind, weakened in will. Baptism remits the guilt; the wounds remain to be healed by grace and struggle.

**Apostolic note**  
“By one man sin entered into this world, and by sin death” (Romans 5:12). Carthage (418) and Trent defined that infants are baptized for the remission of sins because they inherit original sin from Adam.

## 9. Sacraments — *Sacramenta*
**Domain:** Sacraments

**Short**  
Visible signs instituted by Christ that confer the grace they signify.

**Definition**  
The sacraments of the New Law are seven: Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony. They contain the grace they signify and confer it on those who place no obstacle (*ex opere operato*). God is the principal agent; the sacrament is the instrument. The power is Christ’s, not the minister’s personal holiness.

**Apostolic note**  
“Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Ghost…” (John 3:5). Trent Session VII defined the seven sacraments and their efficacy. Aquinas: the principal agent is God; the instrument is the sacrament (ST III, q. 62).

## 10. Scripture & Tradition — *Scriptura et Traditio*
**Domain:** Revelation

**Short**  
The two streams of the one deposit of faith, both to be received and venerated with equal piety and reverence.

**Definition**  
Sacred Scripture is the written Word of God. Sacred Tradition is the living transmission of the Word under the guidance of the Holy Spirit. Neither is complete without the other. The Magisterium is the servant of the Word, not its master; it authentically interprets both Scripture and Tradition for the whole Church.

**Apostolic note**  
“Hold the traditions which you have learned, whether by word or by our epistle” (2 Thessalonians 2:14). Trent and Vatican I refused both a Scripture without Tradition and a Tradition without Scripture. The canon itself is a judgment of the Church.

## 11. Predestination — *Praedestinatio*
**Domain:** Providence / Grace

**Short**  
God’s eternal decree to bring the elect to glory through grace; He predestines no one to evil.

**Definition**  
Predestination is a part of providence: the plan in the divine mind by which some are ordered to eternal life. The cause of predestination is not the foreseen merits of the creature as a debt. The effects of predestination include the merits themselves. God wills all men to be saved and gives sufficient grace. Reprobation is the permission of final impenitence, not a positive decree of damnation independent of demerit.

**Apostolic note**  
“Those he predestinated, he also called” (Romans 8:30). Orange closed the door forever on predestination to evil. Trent refused both presumption and despair. The schools (Thomist, Molinist, Congruist) remain Catholic so long as they hold both poles.

## 12. Faith & Works — *Fides et Opera*
**Domain:** Justification / Moral life

**Short**  
Faith is the beginning and root of justification; works done in grace are its fruit and, under God’s promise, meritorious.

**Definition**  
We are justified by faith, yet not by a faith that remains alone. Living faith is faith formed by charity. Works done before justification do not merit the first gift. Works done in the state of grace are truly the person’s acts, fruits of Christ’s merit, and can increase grace and merit eternal life. When God crowns our merits, He crowns nothing other than His own gifts.

**Apostolic note**  
“Faith without works is dead” (James 2:26). “By grace you are saved through faith… created in Christ Jesus in good works” (Ephesians 2:8–10). Trent holds both verses without letting either drown the other.

**Draft questions**  
1. Are we justified by faith alone?

## 13. Incarnation — *Incarnatio*
**Domain:** Christology

**Short**  
The eternal Word assumed a complete human nature: true God and true man, one Person in two natures.

**Definition**  
In the fullness of time the Son of God, the second Person of the Trinity, assumed flesh of the Virgin Mary by the power of the Holy Spirit. He is consubstantial with the Father according to the Godhead, and consubstantial with us according to the manhood. The union is hypostatic: one Person, two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation.

**Apostolic note**  
“The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us” (John 1:14). The Councils of Ephesus and Chalcedon defined the mystery against Nestorius and Eutyches. “What is not assumed is not healed” (Gregory Nazianzen).

## 14. Baptism — *Baptismus*
**Domain:** Sacraments

**Short**  
The sacrament of regeneration by water and the Holy Spirit: the ordinary gateway of sanctifying grace and incorporation into Christ and the Church.

**Definition**  
Baptism washes away original sin (and actual sin in adults), confers sanctifying grace, imprints an indelible character, and incorporates the person into the Body of Christ. It is necessary for salvation by necessity of precept and of means, yet the Church recognizes baptism of desire and of blood when the rite cannot be had. Infants are baptized because they inherit original sin and are capable of receiving the life they cannot yet profess.

**Apostolic note**  
“Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Ghost, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God” (John 3:5). “One Lord, one faith, one baptism” (Ephesians 4:5). The practice of infant baptism is older than the controversy that later defended it.

## 15. Theosis — *Deificatio / Divinizatio*
**Domain:** Grace / Eschatology

**Short**  
Participation in the divine life without confusion of natures: the creature remains creature, yet is made a partaker of the divine nature.

**Definition**  
Theosis (deification) is the term by which the East, and the Latin tradition in its own key, names the goal of grace. We are made “partakers of the divine nature” (2 Peter 1:4) — not by becoming the divine essence, but by a real participation through the created gift of sanctifying grace and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit. The light of glory in heaven is the last gift, enabling the created mind to see God as He is.

**Apostolic note**  
“We shall see him as he is” (1 John 3:2). Athanasius: “God became man that man might become god” — meaning by grace, not by nature. Aquinas and the Greek Fathers converge: participation is real; identity of essence is not.

## 16. Holy Spirit — *Spiritus Sanctus*
**Domain:** Trinity / Grace

**Short**  
The Lord and Giver of life, who proceeds from the Father and the Son, and who is sent to sanctify the Church and dwell in the justified.

**Definition**  
The Holy Spirit is the third Person of the Trinity, consubstantial with the Father and the Son. He is the Gift, the Love, the Sanctifier. By Him the justified cry “Abba, Father”; by Him the Church is built; by Him the sacraments are made fruitful. Uncreated grace is the Spirit Himself given; created grace is the effect He produces in the soul.

**Apostolic note**  
“I will ask the Father, and he shall give you another Paraclete” (John 14:16). “The charity of God is poured forth in our hearts by the Holy Ghost who is given to us” (Romans 5:5). The *Filioque* expresses the Latin tradition of the Spirit’s procession from the Father and the Son as from one principle.

## Editorial principles for all future articles

1. **Voice** — Apostolic and patristic first; Scholastic precision second; never modern jargon.  
2. **Sources** — Scripture (Douay-Rheims preferred), Councils, Aquinas, Augustine, the Catechism only as summary of the tradition.  
3. **Structure** — Short lede → body paragraphs → gold-bar italic quotes with uppercase sources → sidebar of primary sources.  
4. **Tone** — Neither defensive nor triumphalist. The mystery is greater than the school.  
5. **Unity** — Every concept ultimately points to Christ and the beatific vision.
